💡 Audio Processing 101 for Newbies

What is Audio to a Computer? Audio is just a list of numbers representing air pressure vibrating on a microphone diaphragm. A sample rate of 16 kHz means 16,000 measurements per second.
Waveform vs Spectrogram • Waveform: Shows when sounds are loud or quiet (amplitude in time).
• Spectrogram: Decomposes vibrations into frequencies (Fourier Transform). Low lines = vocal pitch; higher resonant bands = vocal tract shapes (vowels like 'aa', 'ee').
The Core Challenge for ASR Computers cannot read characters directly from 16,000 raw numbers/sec. There are too many noisy vibrations. HuBERT's job is to distill these 16,000 numbers down into meaningful speech representations!

The 320x Temporal Funnel: How 16,000 Samples Become 50 Frames

Raw audio is 1-dimensional and excessively long. HuBERT uses 7 stacked 1D Temporal Convolutions to downsample audio by a factor of 5 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 320.

Handling 10-Second or 1-Minute Speech

For a 10-second sentence (160,000 raw samples), the 1D CNN reduces the sequence length to just 500 feature vectors ($160{,}000 / 320 = 500$). This brings the sequence length well within the quadratic attention budget ($O(T^2)$) of the Transformer!

Convolutional Positional Encoding

Unlike standard Transformers that use sinusoidal embeddings, HuBERT applies a depthwise 1D convolution (kernel size 128, groups 16) directly to the projected features. This provides relative positional awareness that generalizes naturally to arbitrary audio lengths!

How Audio Units Are Managed: The Secret Behind HuBERT

Why is it called Hidden-Unit BERT? Unlike text (where words are already discrete tokens), continuous audio has no spaces or vocabulary. HuBERT invents its own vocabulary by clustering continuous acoustic space into discrete "audio units".

1

Continuous Acoustic Cloud

Every 20ms frame is a point in 512-dimensional continuous feature space. Small acoustic variations (microphone distance, pitch variations) cause points to scatter continuously.

2

k-Means Discrete Quantization

HuBERT runs k-means clustering (e.g. $k=100$) on the representations. Each centroid becomes a discrete pseudo-phoneme token: Unit #12 for nasal onset, Unit #84 for open vowel, etc.

3

Masked Pre-training (BERT-style)

HuBERT masks random spans of audio and forces the Transformer to predict which discrete audio unit belongs in the blank. This forces it to learn acoustic grammar and context!
