Computational Scaling Laws: CNN vs. Transformer

How computational complexity shifts as audio duration grows from 1 second to 60 seconds:

Audio Length Frames GFLOPs CNN Share O(T) Attn Share O(T²)
The Scaling Takeaway: • For short audio (< 10s): The 7-layer 1D CNN accounts for >92% of compute (convolutional filterbanks).
• For long audio (1–10 minutes): The 320x downsampling keeps attention manageable, but the $O(T^2)$ self-attention stage expands rapidly, increasing its share from 6% to over 16%!

Why Foundation Speech Models Need Scale

A deep dive into why self-supervised pre-training (HuBERT) and massive weak supervision (Whisper) achieve superhuman ASR while training CTC from scratch on small datasets struggles.

Scratch CTC (5h – 100h)

The Blank Collapse Dilemma

CTC loss compares all possible alignments of characters interspersed with <blank> tokens. With small datasets and no pre-trained acoustic features, predicting blank for every frame immediately collapses loss from 10.0 to 5.0, trapping gradients in a local minimum before phoneme representations can form.

Meta HuBERT (60,000h)

Self-Supervised Acoustic Clusters

HuBERT uses k-means clustering on intermediate features to create pseudo-phoneme targets, training the Transformer on masked audio prediction (similar to BERT). When fine-tuned on LibriSpeech (960h), the acoustic representations are already rich, yielding an astonishing 1.8% WER with standard CTC.

OpenAI Whisper (680,000h)

Encoder-Decoder Autoregression

Instead of non-autoregressive CTC, Whisper pairs an audio encoder with an autoregressive text decoder, conditioned on prompts, languages, and previous tokens. Trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual internet audio, it acts as a joint acoustic and language model.
